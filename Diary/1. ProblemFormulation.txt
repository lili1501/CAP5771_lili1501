1. What is the central question?

The central question of this project is:

Can ICU mortality be predicted using clinical data collected within 
the first 72 hours of ICU admission?

This is formulated as a supervised binary classification problem, where the goal is to 
estimate the probability of in-hospital mortality using early physiological and 
laboratory indicators.


2. Why is it important in context?
Early identification of high-risk ICU patients is critical for timely intervention, 
resource allocation, and clinical decision-making. Mortality prediction models can 
support clinicians in:

- Recognizing early clinical deterioration
- Prioritizing high-risk patients
- Allocating intensive monitoring and interventions
- Improving overall ICU management strategies

Using only the first 72 hours ensures the model reflects early warning signals 
rather than outcomes influenced by prolonged hospitalization.

3. What would “success” look like?

Success would be:

Developing a model that can reliably distinguish between survivors 
and non-survivors.

Achieving strong discrimination performance (e.g., high AUROC).
Producing clinically interpretable insights into key mortality drivers.
Ensuring no data leakage beyond the 72-hour time window.

A successful model should generalize well and identify physiologically meaningful 
predictors of mortality.

4. What variables are essential, and why?
The essential variables include:

Demographics:

Age, gender, race, ICU type
These provide baseline patient risk context.

Vital Signs (first 72 hours):

Heart rate, MAP, respiratory rate, SpO2, blood pressure
These reflect hemodynamic and respiratory stability.

Laboratory Markers:

Lactate, creatinine, bilirubin, BUN, WBC, platelets, sodium, potassium
These indicate organ dysfunction and systemic stress.

Urine Output:

Total urine output in 72 hours
This reflects renal function and critical illness severity.

These variables are essential because they capture early multi-organ 
physiological status.

5. Who will use the results?

Potential users include:

- ICU clinicians and hospital staff
- Clinical data scientists
- Hospital administrators
- Researchers studying critical care outcomes

The results may inform risk stratification strategies and support 
evidence-based ICU management.

Is this realistic given constraints?

Yes, this project is realistic within the defined constraints:

The dataset (MIMIC-IV) is structured and accessible.
All features are restricted to the first 72 hours, preventing leakage.
Aggregated summaries reduce computational complexity.
The modeling task is clearly defined and measurable.
However, constraints include class imbalance, missing laboratory data, 
and single-center data limitations.

Overall, the project is feasible and appropriate given available resources and scope.
