## What features did I engineer or transform, and why?

### 1. Race Consolidation
**Before:** The `race` column contained over 20 highly granular categories including entries like "WHITE - EASTERN EUROPEAN", "WHITE - OTHER EUROPEAN", "BLACK/AFRICAN AMERICAN", "BLACK/CAPE VERDEAN", etc.

**After:** Consolidated into 6 clean categories: White, Black, Asian, Hispanic, Native American, Other/Unknown.

**Why:** The granular categories had very small counts per group, making them statistically unreliable as model features. Consolidating reduces noise while preserving meaningful demographic signal. Entries containing "UNABLE TO OBTAIN" and "UNKNOWN" were mapped to "Other/Unknown" to avoid imputing demographic information.

```python
def clean_race(race):
    race = race.upper()
    if "WHITE" in race:     return "White"
    elif "BLACK" in race:   return "Black"
    elif "ASIAN" in race:   return "Asian"
    elif "HISPANIC" in race or "LATINO" in race: return "Hispanic"
    elif "AMERICAN INDIAN" in race: return "Native American"
    else:                   return "Other/Unknown"
```

### 2. Care Unit Standardisation
**Before:** `first_careunit` contained long descriptive strings like "Medical Intensive Care Unit (MICU)", "Cardiac Vascular Intensive Care Unit (CVICU)".

**After:** Cleaned to standardised short labels using a mapping function `clean_careunit()`.

**Why:** Consistent labels are required for one-hot encoding during modelling. The long strings also caused display issues in visualisations.

### 3. Admission Location Standardisation
**Before:** `admission_location` had inconsistent capitalisation and verbose descriptions.

**After:** Cleaned and standardised via `clean_admission_location()`.

**Why:** Same reason as care unit — consistency for encoding and downstream analysis.

### 4. No Feature Scaling Applied
I deliberately chose not to apply StandardScaler or MinMaxScaler to the numeric features.

**Why:** The chosen models (Random Forest, HistGradientBoosting, XGBoost) are all tree-based and do not require feature scaling — they are invariant to monotonic transformations of features. Scaling would add complexity with no benefit. SMOTE, which does use distance calculations, operates on the preprocessed data after the pipeline's internal scaler handles that step.

## What did I remove and why?

### Removed: `stay_id`
**Justification:** Unique identifier for each ICU stay. Contains no predictive information — it is only used for relational joins. Including it would cause the model to memorise stay IDs rather than learn clinical patterns.

### Removed: `race` (original column)
**Justification:** Replaced by `race_clean` (the consolidated version). The original column was redundant after cleaning.

### Removed: `admission_location` (original column)
**Justification:** Replaced by `admission_loc` (the standardised version).

**Justification:** Replaced by `careunit` (the standardised version).

## What risks remain?

### Encoding introduces dimensionality
One-hot encoding of `race_clean`, `careunit`, and `admission_loc` will expand the feature space. With 6 race categories, 7 care units, and 6 admission locations, approximately 17 additional binary columns are created. This is manageable but should be monitored.

### Temporal features not engineered
Mean/min/max aggregates do not capture temporal trends. A patient deteriorating over 72h has the same mean as a patient improving. Future work could include trend features (e.g., last 24h mean vs first 24h mean).

### Fairness risk with race feature
Including `race_clean` as a feature risks encoding systemic healthcare inequalities. If Black patients historically received less aggressive treatment, the model may associate race with mortality due to differential care rather than underlying physiology. This is an important ethical consideration for clinical deployment.
